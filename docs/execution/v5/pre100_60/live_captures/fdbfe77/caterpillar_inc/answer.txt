The answer Across the public record for Caterpillar Inc., fixed cost is committed in large increments against demand that arrives in small ones. That matters because Caterpillar Inc. appears to be committing capital to capacity ahead of demand it does not control, concentrating the outcome in a small number of buyers' product cycles. What most limits this: utilisation, order books and take-or-pay terms are not public. Why this matters now This is strategically important but not newly urgent. Nothing in the public record carries a date that marks a change — the material is dated to when it was read, not to when anything happened. The question therefore stands on the structure of the business rather than on a recent event, which is an argument for settling it deliberately rather than quickly. What changed No dated change could be established — read that as a limit on this analysis and not as a finding about the company. A change may well have happened where nothing published records it. What this costs or wins Sales cycles Caterpillar Inc. appears to depend on regulated or public-sector buyers in a way that shapes the business: it is bought through public procurement machinery rather than ordinary sales. Purchasing of that kind moves on budget and accreditation cycles the company cannot influence. No retrieved source states this in its own words, so treat it as a reading of the evidence rather than something the company has said. The decision this bears on The choice: Commit to the reading now versus hold and verify first.

The answer Across the public record for Exxon Mobil Corporation, fixed cost is committed in large increments against demand that arrives in small ones. That matters because Exxon Mobil Corporation appears to be committing capital to capacity ahead of demand it does not control, concentrating the outcome in a small number of buyers' product cycles. What most limits this: utilisation, order books and take-or-pay terms are not public. Why this matters now This is strategically important but not newly urgent. Nothing in the public record carries a date that marks a change — the material is dated to when it was read, not to when anything happened. The question therefore stands on the structure of the business rather than on a recent event, which is an argument for settling it deliberately rather than quickly. What changed No dated change could be established — read that as a limit on this analysis and not as a finding about the company. A change may well have happened where nothing published records it. It bears on one choice: whether to shift pricing toward smaller committed steps, and whether to fund growth from operating cash rather than debt. Competitive pressure The alternatives this company's own evidence names are LM4. The closest is LM4 — competes for the same buyer with a comparable product. Stated in the source as: “To the extent that we have available capacity on our gathering systems, at TGP or LM4 for third-party volumes, we may not be able to compete effectively with third‑party gathering systems or processing plants for additional natural gas production in the area.” It bears on one choice: whether the differentiation this plan assumes is one a buyer comparing the two would actually notice. Outside conditions bound this decision; they do not make it. A share-price move records what the market expects, not whether the strategy is working.

2015 — as the record stood in February 2016

What was true thenObserved

By February 2016 the record showed 3 published financial year(s), the most recent 2015 at an index of 133, earned at a 25.8% operating margin — growing 14.4% a year. That is what a decision at this date had to work from.

What management didObserved

The choice is legible in the figures it filed: revenue growth slowed while the margin widened — the shape of a business taking operating leverage. For this kind of business the levers available were engagement and time spent, ad load and format mix and targeting and measurement quality.

What the market expectedModelled

On the record as it stood in February 2016, continuing at 15.3% and reverting toward 12% would have put the index near 442 by 2025. This is modelled from information published by that date — it is not a retrieved consensus and no analyst said it.

What happenedObserved

By 2025 the index reached 750 — 70% ahead of what the record at the time implied. This card is the only one on this page built from material filed after February 2016.

Better alternativeCounterfactual

Redeploy the margin now being earned into the next growth vector, before the current one finishes decaying. The same attention sold for more, with no capital spent.

Why it might have workedCounterfactual

Price per impression is set by how many advertisers bid for the same person, so bringing more bidders — smaller advertisers, new formats, better measurement — lifts the price on inventory that already exists, at no incremental cost of supply.

What could have invalidated itCounterfactual

A business that has just learned to earn a margin is being asked to spend it again, and the market rewarded the margin. The assumption it rests on is that there is a next vector to fund, which the company's own investment and product disclosure at the time either shows or does not — and that was checkable at the time, which is what makes this an argument rather than a preference.

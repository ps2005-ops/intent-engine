2009 — as the record stood in February 2010

What was true thenObserved

By February 2010 the record showed 3 published financial year(s), the most recent 2009 at an index of 111, earned at a 30.1% operating margin — shrinking 1.3% a year. That is what a decision at this date had to work from.

What management didObserved

The choice is legible in the figures it filed: revenue growth slowed while the margin widened — the shape of a business taking operating leverage. For this kind of business the levers available were pricing and promotion, product mix and innovation and channel and distribution strategy.

What the market expectedModelled

On the record as it stood in February 2010, continuing at 5.3% and reverting toward 4% would have put the index near 212 by 2025. This is modelled from information published by that date — it is not a retrieved consensus and no analyst said it.

What happenedObserved

By 2025 the index reached 147 — 31% behind what the record at the time implied. This card is the only one on this page built from material filed after February 2010.

Better alternativeCounterfactual

Redeploy the margin now being earned into the next growth vector, before the current one finishes decaying. Real revenue held and margin protected through the cost spike.

Why it might have workedCounterfactual

Brand strength is the ability to raise price without losing volume; taking it early in an inflationary period holds real revenue, and taking it late means absorbing the cost increase in margin first.

What could have invalidated itCounterfactual

A business that has just learned to earn a margin is being asked to spend it again, and the market rewarded the margin. The assumption it rests on is that there is a next vector to fund, which the company's own investment and product disclosure at the time either shows or does not — and that was checkable at the time, which is what makes this an argument rather than a preference.

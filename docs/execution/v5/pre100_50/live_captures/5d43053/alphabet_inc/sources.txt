The answer Across the public record for Alphabet Inc., when agents transact, the rails they call matter more than the human-facing storefront. That matters because Alphabet Inc. appears to be positioning for buying that is mediated by AI agents rather than by people directly, shifting where demand is captured. The company's own words: “In Other Bets, our fully autonomous driving technology company, Waymo, is now providing fully autonomous, paid ride-hailing services to customers in multiple cities.” — SEC 10-K (2026-02-05). What most limits this: real agent-driven order volume vs. human browsing is not yet observable in public data. Why this matters now This is strategically important but not newly urgent. Nothing in the public record carries a date that marks a change — the material is dated to when it was read, not to when anything happened. The question therefore stands on the structure of the business rather than on a recent event, which is an argument for settling it deliberately rather than quickly. What changed No dated change could be established — read that as a limit on this analysis and not as a finding about the company. A change may well have happened where nothing published records it.

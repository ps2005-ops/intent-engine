# Positioning — decision-support memo (analysis only)

*No code, no marketing copy, no accuracy claims. A thesis to develop and
pressure-test, for the founder's strategic decision. The load-bearing
constraint throughout: the system has **0 resolved ledger predictions**,
so any positioning that leans on an accuracy number is not available to us
yet — and this memo argues that's a feature of the position, not a
weakness to paper over.*

## Thesis

The defensible position is **not a higher accuracy number**. It is
**verified, code-graded, honestly-calibrated forward prediction with
transparent method and visible uncertainty.** Every competitor can quote
an impressive number; almost none can show you a public, append-only,
code-graded ledger of forward calls — including their misses — measured
against dumb baselines. That gap is the moat, and it is a *trust* moat,
not a *performance* moat.

## (a) What incumbents actually measure — and why none of it equals verified forward market calibration

- **FP&A / "AI analyst" tools** (planning copilots): measure
  *internal-plan-vs-actuals* — did the company hit the number it set. That
  is variance reporting, not prediction of an external market; the bar is
  set by the customer and the "accuracy" is about their own forecasting
  process, not the tool's causal edge.
- **Trading / signal AIs**: quote *backtested hit-rates* — performance on
  historical data the model (or its designers) could see. Backtests are
  notoriously flattering (overfitting, survivorship, look-ahead, and — for
  LLMs — plain memory of what happened). A backtested number is an
  in-sample or quasi-in-sample claim dressed as an out-of-sample one.
- **Sell-side / research-style outputs**: measured, if at all, by
  narrative plausibility and post-hoc rationalization; rarely a
  pre-registered, dated, falsifiable claim graded by code.

None of these is *verified forward market calibration*: a claim registered
BEFORE the outcome, dated, graded mechanically against real data on its
resolve date, with hits and misses both kept. That is the specific thing
our ledger does and theirs generally do not.

## (b) Why our honesty markers + code-grading + baselines are the differentiator

- **Honesty markers** (UNAVAILABLE labels, "none matched — and that's the
  finding", loud DATA GAPS): they signal a system that reports the absence
  of signal, which is exactly what a trustworthy analyst does and a
  hype-driven one never does.
- **Code-grading, append-only**: we cannot quietly delete a miss. The
  ledger's integrity is structural, not promised. That is checkable by a
  skeptical buyer.
- **Baselines**: every engine claim is measured against deliberately dumb
  rules (momentum, base-rate). "We beat a coin-flip baseline, here are the
  numbers" is a fundamentally different (and more credible) statement than
  "our accuracy is 87%."
- **Explanation depth** (T007, spec pending): a *traceable* why — matched
  conditions + cited historical precedent + causal chain — not a
  generated narrative. Competitors' explanations are usually post-hoc
  story; ours is a render of deterministic, cited data.

The synthesis: the product's claim is **"here is our method, here is what
we can't verify, here is our forward record as it accumulates, graded by
code you can inspect."** That is defensible precisely because it is hard to
fake and easy to check.

## (c) The one thing we CANNOT do before calibration — and why faking it is fatal

Until ~late-Aug/Sep 2026 (first resolutions) and enough resolved
predictions to measure calibration (A-M5: ≥30/source + your review), **we
cannot honestly claim an accuracy or edge number.** The temptation, under
competitive pressure, will be to borrow one — a backtest figure, a
cherry-picked example, a "typically ~X%." Doing so would **destroy the
exact asset that is the moat**: the moment we quote an unverified number,
we become indistinguishable from every incumbent whose numbers we're
arguing can't be trusted — and worse, we'd have contradicted our own
central claim in the first sentence of the pitch. The honesty *is* the
product; a single faked number converts it back into commodity hype. The
discipline of saying "we don't have that number yet, and here's why that
honesty is the point" is not a marketing liability to endure — it is the
demonstration of the trait we're selling.

## (d) What we CAN sell right now (no accuracy claim required)

1. **Transparent structural analysis**: which documented mechanisms match
   a founder's situation, matched deterministically, with cited historical
   precedent — and honest silence when nothing matches.
2. **Explanation depth** (once T007 ships): the traceable *why* behind
   each match — a genuinely differentiated artifact vs. narrative
   "analyst" output.
3. **Visible-uncertainty reporting**: the founder-readable report whose
   caveats are features.
4. **A front-row seat to the calibration as it accumulates**: early users
   watch the ledger fill and resolve in public — they are buying into the
   trust story at the ground floor, which is itself a compelling early-
   adopter pitch ("watch us earn the number instead of asserting it").

## Positioning one-liner (for pressure-testing, not final copy)

*"Most market-analysis AI tells you a confident number you can't check.
We show you the mechanism, the historical precedent, what we can't verify,
and a public forward record graded by code — and we won't quote you an
accuracy figure until we've earned one."*

## What would change this memo

The first real calibration read (post-late-Aug). If the engine genuinely
beats its baselines over ≥30 resolved predictions, the position GAINS a
verified number to stand on — but note the number becomes *supporting
evidence for the trust claim*, not a replacement for it. The moat stays
the verifiability, not the digit.

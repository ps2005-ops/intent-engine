2018 — as the record stood in February 2019

What was true thenObserved

By February 2019 the record showed 3 published financial year(s), the most recent 2018 at an index of 113 — growing 6.7% a year. That is what a decision at this date had to work from.

What management didObserved

The choice is legible in the figures it filed: revenue growth accelerated. For this kind of business the levers available were pipeline and development priorities, regulatory and approval strategy and pricing and market access.

What the market expectedModelled

On the record as it stood in February 2019, continuing at 6.5% and reverting toward 4% would have put the index near 158 by 2025. This is modelled from information published by that date — it is not a retrieved consensus and no analyst said it.

What happenedObserved

By 2025 the index reached 131 — 17% behind what the record at the time implied. This card is the only one on this page built from material filed after February 2019.

Better alternativeCounterfactual

Press the advantage: fund the next expansion while the cost base is still absorbing the current one. A replacement on the market before the cliff rather than after it.

Why it might have workedCounterfactual

Revenue from an approved product is contractual until exclusivity lapses and then falls quickly; the only thing that replaces it is a product funded years before, so the decision and its consequence sit in different decades.

What could have invalidated itCounterfactual

An expansion funded on the strength of one good year is a bet that the year was structural, and a cycle turning underneath it is exactly what makes it look structural. The assumption it rests on is that the cost base absorbing this year's growth will absorb next year's, which holds while the margin is widening and stops the moment it is not — and that was checkable at the time, which is what makes this an argument rather than a preference.

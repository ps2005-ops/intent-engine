2009 — as the record stood in March 2010

What was true thenObserved

By March 2010 the record showed 3 published financial year(s), the most recent 2009 at an index of 185, earned at a 8.8% operating margin — growing 24.6% a year. That is what a decision at this date had to work from.

What management didObserved

The choice is legible in the figures it filed: revenue growth slowed while the margin widened — the shape of a business taking operating leverage. For this kind of business the levers available were pricing and packaging, sales motion and coverage and retention and expansion programmes.

What the market expectedModelled

On the record as it stood in March 2010, continuing at 35.9% and reverting toward 15% would have put the index near 3179 by 2025. This is modelled from information published by that date — it is not a retrieved consensus and no analyst said it.

What happenedObserved

By 2025 the index reached 6483 — 104% ahead of what the record at the time implied. This card is the only one on this page built from material filed after March 2010.

Better alternativeCounterfactual

Redeploy the margin now being earned into the next growth vector, before the current one finishes decaying. The same revenue path at a higher margin, compounding.

Why it might have workedCounterfactual

The cost base of a subscription business is largely fixed, so holding hiring flat while contracted revenue keeps compounding turns the same top line into a materially higher margin — and the index here counts the margin the growth was earned at.

What could have invalidated itCounterfactual

A business that has just learned to earn a margin is being asked to spend it again, and the market rewarded the margin. The assumption it rests on is that there is a next vector to fund, which the company's own investment and product disclosure at the time either shows or does not — and that was checkable at the time, which is what makes this an argument rather than a preference.

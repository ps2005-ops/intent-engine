2012 — as the record stood in March 2013

What was true thenObserved

What management didObserved

The choice is legible in the figures it filed: revenue growth slowed and the margin narrowed with it — cost was not coming out as fast as demand. For this kind of business the levers available were pricing and packaging, sales motion and coverage and retention and expansion programmes.

What the market expectedModelled

What happenedObserved

Better alternativeCounterfactual

Stop paying for growth that has already stopped arriving, and take the cost out before the market prices the slowdown in. The same revenue path at a higher margin, compounding.

Why it might have workedCounterfactual

The cost base of a subscription business is largely fixed, so holding hiring flat while contracted revenue keeps compounding turns the same top line into a materially higher margin — and the index here counts the margin the growth was earned at.

What could have invalidated itCounterfactual

Cutting into a cyclical trough removes the capacity needed to serve the recovery, and capacity of this kind takes a year to rebuild. The assumption it rests on is that the slowdown is structural rather than a single weak period — the distinction the whole decision turns on, and one the next two quarters settle — and that was checkable at the time, which is what makes this an argument rather than a preference.

Better alternativeCounterfactual

Press the advantage: fund the next expansion while the cost base is still absorbing the current one. The same revenue path at a higher margin, compounding.

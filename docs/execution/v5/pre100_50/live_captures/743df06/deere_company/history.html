2010 — as the record stood in December 2010

What was true thenObserved

By December 2010 the record showed 4 published financial year(s), the most recent 2010 at an index of 122, earned at a 13.1% operating margin — growing 19.0% a year. That is what a decision at this date had to work from.

What management didObserved

The choice is legible in the figures it filed: revenue growth accelerated and the margin widened with it — growth the cost base was absorbing. For this kind of business the levers available were production rate, supply chain qualification and programme and certification management.

What the market expectedModelled

On the record as it stood in December 2010, continuing at 6.9% and reverting toward 4% would have put the index near 227 by 2025. This is modelled from information published by that date — it is not a retrieved consensus and no analyst said it.

What happenedObserved

By 2025 the index reached 190 — 16% behind what the record at the time implied. This card is the only one on this page built from material filed after December 2010.

Better alternativeCounterfactual

Press the advantage: fund the next expansion while the cost base is still absorbing the current one. A shallower trough, and a margin that improves through it because aftermarket work carries a higher one.

Why it might have workedCounterfactual

The installed base generates parts and service revenue that does not fall with new-equipment demand, so weighting the sales and service organisation toward it before the downturn holds revenue up exactly when the cycle is taking it down.

What could have invalidated itCounterfactual

An expansion funded on the strength of one good year is a bet that the year was structural, and a cycle turning underneath it is exactly what makes it look structural. The assumption it rests on is that the cost base absorbing this year's growth will absorb next year's, which holds while the margin is widening and stops the moment it is not — and that was checkable at the time, which is what makes this an argument rather than a preference.

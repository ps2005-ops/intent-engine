Executive brief — the decision memo

The answer

The decision in front of management is what to charge, and for what, without losing more volume than the price gains?

Causal confidence: BOUNDED — supported in direction, not in size.

What is known: branded product sold through retail and direct channels, where the brand carries pricing power the product alone would not.

What remains unknown: volume and price/mix split is not disclosed at the granularity the decision needs.

Why that matters: it is the parameter that decides whether a pricing action is worth making now or worth deferring, and the two answers lead to different plans.

What management should do now: move on pricing and promotion at a size that can be reversed inside one planning cycle, and instrument it so the result is readable before the next commitment.

Minimum viable experiment: run the change on one segment or one cohort, hold the rest constant, and read volume against the unchanged group for one full cycle — that isolates the effect without committing the whole book.

Kill switch: if the direction is not visible by the next review, the assumption under the decision has failed.

What would change our mind: a disclosure in pricing and promotional actions showing the mechanism moving in the opposite direction would overturn this reading.

What this run could not establish on its own: no curated transition pattern matched PepsiCo, Inc. in this pass, so nothing here rests on one.

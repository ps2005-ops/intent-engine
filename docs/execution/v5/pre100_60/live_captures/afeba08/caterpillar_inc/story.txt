Where the money actually comes from Revenue is decided by orders and backlog, delivery or production rate and aftermarket and services on the installed base, and whether that revenue becomes margin is decided by supply chain and component availability and labour and skills. There is no other lever on the top line. Why growth and profitability are one question HIGH against production rate: fixed cost is absorbed by rate, so a rate change moves margin more than revenue. So a decision that moves volume moves margin with it, and the two cannot be planned separately here. What the market rewards It sells into duopoly or oligopoly with certification and installed-base barriers that make entry slow and displacement rare, where the contest is most direct with CNH Industrial N.V, Deere Construction and Independent service and will-fit parts. Where the strategy is exposed fixed cost is committed in large increments against demand that arrives in small ones. The lever management holds against that is production rate. What the argument is actually about fixed cost is committed in large increments against demand that arrives in small ones. Utilisation, order books and take-or-pay terms are not public. it is the parameter that decides whether a capacity commitment is worth making now or worth deferring, and the two answers lead to different plans. What the other side does Caterpillar Inc. names it as a competitor in its own filing — examples of global competitors include CASE (part of CNH Industrial N.V.), Deere Construction & Forestry (part of Deere & Company), Doosan Bobcat (Part of Doosan Group), Hitachi Co. CNH Industrial N.V matches on capability rather than on headline price, because a public price cut is visible to its whole installed base and a feature is not. The counter available here is hold list price and compete on the part of the job they have to build rather than buy. and the thing to watch for is discounting that appears in their public pricing rather than in individual deals.

2009 — as the record stood in January 2010

What was true thenObserved

By January 2010 the record showed 3 published financial year(s), the most recent 2009 at an index of 166, earned at a 4.6% operating margin — growing 28.1% a year. That is what a decision at this date had to work from.

What management didObserved

The choice is legible in the figures it filed: revenue growth slowed while the margin widened — the shape of a business taking operating leverage. For this kind of business the levers available were capital allocation between engines, cross-engine bundling and pricing per engine.

What the market expectedModelled

On the record as it stood in January 2010, continuing at 28.7% and reverting toward 10% would have put the index near 1157 by 2025. This is modelled from information published by that date — it is not a retrieved consensus and no analyst said it.

What happenedObserved

By 2025 the index reached 5145 — 345% ahead of what the record at the time implied. This card is the only one on this page built from material filed after January 2010.

Better alternativeCounterfactual

Redeploy the margin now being earned into the next growth vector, before the current one finishes decaying. A materially higher margin on a similar revenue path.

Why it might have workedCounterfactual

The engines earn very different margins on the same capital, so moving investment toward the infrastructure engine raises operating profit faster than the same investment spread across the whole — and the index here counts profit, not revenue.

What could have invalidated itCounterfactual

A business that has just learned to earn a margin is being asked to spend it again, and the market rewarded the margin. The assumption it rests on is that there is a next vector to fund, which the company's own investment and product disclosure at the time either shows or does not — and that was checkable at the time, which is what makes this an argument rather than a preference.

  • reversion toward 10% because the engines do not turn together: a commerce engine mean-reverts with the consumer while an infrastructure engine compounds on contracts, so the consolidated line reverts more slowly than retail and faster than software
  • a band from this company's own realised volatility (12.6 points a year)

Press the advantage: fund the next expansion while the cost base is still absorbing the current one. A materially higher margin on a similar revenue path.

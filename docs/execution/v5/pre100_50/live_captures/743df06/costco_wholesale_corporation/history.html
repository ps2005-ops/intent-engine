2010 — as the record stood in October 2010

What was true thenObserved

By October 2010 the record showed 3 published financial year(s), the most recent 2010 at an index of 107, earned at a 2.7% operating margin — growing 9.3% a year. That is what a decision at this date had to work from.

What management didObserved

The choice is legible in the figures it filed: revenue growth accelerated and the margin widened with it — growth the cost base was absorbing. For this kind of business the levers available were pricing and promotion, product mix and innovation and channel and distribution strategy.

What happenedObserved

By 2025 the index reached 384 — 99% ahead of what the record at the time implied. This card is the only one on this page built from material filed after October 2010.

Better alternativeCounterfactual

Press the advantage: fund the next expansion while the cost base is still absorbing the current one. Real revenue held and margin protected through the cost spike.

Why it might have workedCounterfactual

Brand strength is the ability to raise price without losing volume; taking it early in an inflationary period holds real revenue, and taking it late means absorbing the cost increase in margin first.

What could have invalidated itCounterfactual

An expansion funded on the strength of one good year is a bet that the year was structural, and a cycle turning underneath it is exactly what makes it look structural. The assumption it rests on is that the cost base absorbing this year's growth will absorb next year's, which holds while the margin is widening and stops the moment it is not — and that was checkable at the time, which is what makes this an argument rather than a preference.

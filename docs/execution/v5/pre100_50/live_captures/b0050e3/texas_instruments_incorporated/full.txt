The answer Across the public record for Texas Instruments Incorporated, fixed cost is committed in large increments against demand that arrives in small ones. That matters because Texas Instruments Incorporated appears to be committing capital to capacity ahead of demand it does not control, concentrating the outcome in a small number of buyers' product cycles. What most limits this: the company's filings carry this, so it is stated under legal obligation rather than in marketing — but no outside account has tested whether it is working. Why this matters now This is strategically important but not newly urgent. Nothing in the public record carries a date that marks a change — the material is dated to when it was read, not to when anything happened. The question therefore stands on the structure of the business rather than on a recent event, which is an argument for settling it deliberately rather than quickly. What changed No dated change could be established — read that as a limit on this analysis and not as a finding about the company. A change may well have happened where nothing published records it.

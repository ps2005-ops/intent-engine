Where the money actually comes from Revenue is decided by approved products and indications, volume and prescriptions or procedures and net price after rebates, and whether that revenue becomes margin is decided by research and development and clinical and trial cost. There is no other lever on the top line. Why growth and profitability are one question HIGH per approved product: development cost is sunk before the first sale and marginal supply cost is comparatively small. So a decision that moves volume moves margin with it, and the two cannot be planned separately here. What the market rewards It sells into protected positions of limited life: approval and exclusivity confer pricing power until they expire, where the contest is most direct with Keytruda Qlex, loss of exclusivity on Bridion and loss of exclusivity on Janumet. Where the strategy is exposed The open question is which development programmes to fund and which to stop, given how long it takes a programme to reach approved products and indications. The lever management holds is pipeline and development priorities, and the cost of pulling it is that the effect is visible to competitors before it is measurable internally.

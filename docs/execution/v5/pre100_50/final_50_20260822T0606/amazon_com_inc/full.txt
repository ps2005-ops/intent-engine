The workflow that Amazon.com, Inc.'s product sits inside may stop existing rather than be improved. It sells its stores to enable hundreds of millions of unique products to be sold by us and by third parties across dozens of product categories. A product priced against a human process captures a share of that process's cost; when the process is performed by a machine end to end, the customer is no longer buying a better way to do it, and there is nothing left for the price to be a share of. Why this is usually missed: competitive analysis compares against firms selling the same shape of product, and a workflow disappearing does not appear in any of them. For it: the product is described in terms of the process it supports rather than the outcome it delivers Against it: a product priced against a measurable OUTCOME survives the process being automated and would contradict this It is falsified by customers continuing to buy the process-shaped product as automation spreads falsifies this.

The workflow that Alphabet Inc.'s product sits inside may stop existing rather than be improved. It sells to its Google Cloud customers. A product priced against a human process captures a share of that process's cost; when the process is performed by a machine end to end, the customer is no longer buying a better way to do it, and there is nothing left for the price to be a share of. Why this is usually missed: competitive analysis compares against firms selling the same shape of product, and a workflow disappearing does not appear in any of them. For it: the product is described in terms of the process it supports rather than the outcome it delivers Against it: a product priced against a measurable OUTCOME survives the process being automated and would contradict this It is falsified by customers continuing to buy the process-shaped product as automation spreads falsifies this. Smallest test: offer one cohort outcome-based pricing and measure take-up against the existing basis. Standing: LIVE. Alphabet Inc.'s most important competitor may be its own customers deciding to do this themselves. It charges on consumption-based fees and subscriptions for infrastructure, platform, applications, and other cloud services. A charge that scales with the customer's own volume grows into a line item large enough to justify a build, and the largest customers reach that threshold first — which are the same accounts the revenue depends on most. Why this is usually missed: A customer who builds is recorded as churn, not as a competitive loss, so this never enters the competitive picture at all. For it: the pricing basis — consumption-based fees and subscriptions for infrastructure, platform, applications, and other cloud services — scales with the customer rather than with what it costs to serve them Against it: a capability the customer cannot assemble — regulated, network-dependent, or data-dependent — would rule this out It is falsified by largest accounts showing no build activity at renewal falsifies this.

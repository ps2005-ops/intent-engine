2009 — as the record stood in February 2010

What was true thenObserved

By February 2010 the record showed 3 published financial year(s), the most recent 2009 at an index of 72, earned at a 19.1% operating margin — shrinking 16.9% a year. That is what a decision at this date had to work from.

What management didObserved

The choice is legible in the figures it filed: revenue growth slowed and the margin narrowed with it — cost was not coming out as fast as demand. For this kind of business the levers available were capacity commitment, product roadmap and mix and pricing and supply agreements.

What the market expectedModelled

On the record as it stood in February 2010, continuing at -15.4% and reverting toward 6% would have put the index near 141 by 2025. This is modelled from information published by that date — it is not a retrieved consensus and no analyst said it.

What happenedObserved

By 2025 the index reached 137 — 3% behind what the record at the time implied. This card is the only one on this page built from material filed after February 2010.

Better alternativeCounterfactual

Stop paying for growth that has already stopped arriving, and take the cost out before the market prices the slowdown in. A share of the next product generation rather than the current one.

Why it might have workedCounterfactual

A design win locks revenue for the life of the product, and the win is decided by what is available when the customer designs — so capacity and process readiness a year early converts into revenue years later.

What could have invalidated itCounterfactual

Cutting into a cyclical trough removes the capacity needed to serve the recovery, and capacity of this kind takes a year to rebuild. The assumption it rests on is that the slowdown is structural rather than a single weak period — the distinction the whole decision turns on, and one the next two quarters settle — and that was checkable at the time, which is what makes this an argument rather than a preference.

Better alternativeCounterfactual

Press the advantage: fund the next expansion while the cost base is still absorbing the current one. A share of the next product generation rather than the current one.

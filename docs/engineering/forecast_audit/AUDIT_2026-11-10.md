## 1. The answer in one paragraph

No forecast's deadline was ever moved. "September", "October" and "December"
are the first-due dates of **three different cohorts**, each told correctly at
the time. The September/October cohort (17 predictions, July 2026) did come due
and was processed, but its resolver closed all six FRED-based forecasts as
"unresolvable" at 06:00 on their due dates, before the values were published,
and its generator has produced nothing since 2026-07-20. Nothing downstream ever
read its scores. The production market runtime has been learning from evidence
(45 reconciliations), but its price-outcome step is a stub that has graded none
of the 502 records now past horizon. December 11 is correct for the 977-forecast
cohort and for nothing older.

## 6. What changed in code (this branch only)

**July ledger** (`core/prediction_ledger.py`, `core/market_resolution.py`,
`core/macro_data.py`, `scripts/resolve_market_predictions.py`):

- Reads take the forecast terms (`created_at` = information cutoff, `resolve_by`,
  probability, rule, horizon, instrument, source) from the **registration row**.
  Later rows contribute only the outcome. The write path refuses a row that
  changes those terms (`LedgerIntegrityError`), and `immutable_drift()` names any
  such row written by other means.
- Data that is missing because it isn't published yet, or because a source is down,
  is now `pending`, never persisted, and retried daily until a fixed final date (due
  date + the rule's own search window + publication allowance: 85 days for FRED,
  15 for Tiingo). Only after that is it terminally `unresolvable`, and the note
  names the original due date.
- Each look at a prediction is logged in a separate `resolution_attempts` table
  (`as_of`, `original_resolve_by`, `status`, `next_attempt_on`). Retry dates live
  there and never on the prediction.
- Empty FRED and Tiingo answers are no longer cached.

**Paper ledger** (`paper_validation/schedule.py` new, `ledger.py`, `forward.py`):

- The schedule comes from a rule-based NYSE calendar that reproduces all 6,501
  SPY sessions from 2001 to 2026, not from counting bars in today's feed.
- New registrations seal `scheduled_entry_session`, `scheduled_exit_session` and
  `schedule_calendar` into the hash chain. A registration whose stored dates
  disagree with the calendar is refused. The 977 existing records are recomputed
  from the same rules, and a test pins them to 2026-11-10 / 2026-12-10 / 2026-12-11.
- The ledger refuses a resolution on any other session, and refuses a VOID before
  the window closes. The resolver waits on a stale feed (a fetch failure never
  closes a forecast), voids with the reason when a scheduled session has no bar,
  and records `benchmark_sessions_elapsed`.
- `status` now reports each cohort's due date from the registrations
  (`due_schedule`), so every surface quotes the same date.

**Tests:** `tests/test_forecast_deadline_immutability.py`, 24 tests across
restart, redeploy (rerun on a later clock), regenerate, migrate (copy and edit),
the September defect, stale feeds, delisting, the calendar, and the market
store's window guard. Each of the 10 fixes was reverted one at a time in a
scratch copy, and every revert was caught by its own test. Existing tests
changed: two assertions that encoded the old behaviour (a level rule due in the
future expected "unresolvable"; a no-data case created today expected it
immediately), one stub signature, and the ledger test helper, which now carries
a real schedule instead of `"x"`. 399 tests across the 31 affected files pass.

## 7. Verdict

- **Is December 11 legitimate?** Yes, for the 977-forecast cohort registered
  2026-11-09. It isn't a postponement of anything.
- **Have older forecasts been lost or postponed?** None postponed. Six were
  wrongly closed and are recoverable (outcomes in the audit file). 45 econ-fork
  expectations have no resolver. 502 signal records were never graded by
  production (369 graded here).
- **How many real forward predictions should already be resolved?** 14 (the
  July cohort's machine-resolvable ones). All 14 now have an outcome. About four
  of the ten engine forecasts among them are clean tests.
- **Has the learning system been functioning?** Partly. Evidence-driven belief
  revision in the market runtime works. Forward price-forecast learning has not
  happened: one small cohort came due, its scores reached nothing, and its
  generator stopped in July.

Where this has played out before

Capacity is bought in large, slow increments while demand arrives in small, fast ones. Committing early wins share when the forecast holds and strands fixed cost when it does not — and the forecast usually rests on a handful of large customers whose own product cycles set the timing.

Where the comparison stops: Capacity is rented or elastic, or demand is spread across many independent buyers. Check that against the company before relying on the analogy — it is the one condition that would make it the wrong comparison.

Seen at: Memory and sensor fabrication cycles — capacity added on forecast, then written down when handset demand moved; Contract manufacturing capacity build-outs — multi-year fab commitments against customer roadmaps

2009 — as the record stood in January 2010

What was true thenObserved

By January 2010 the record showed 3 published financial year(s), the most recent 2009 at an index of 166, earned at a 4.6% operating margin — growing 28.1% a year. That is what a decision at this date had to work from.

What management didObserved

The choice is legible in the figures it filed: revenue growth slowed while the margin widened — the shape of a business taking operating leverage. For this kind of business the levers available were sourcing and private label, inventory and turns and distribution density.

What the market expectedModelled

On the record as it stood in January 2010, continuing at 28.7% and reverting toward 3% would have put the index near 380 by 2025. This is modelled from information published by that date — it is not a retrieved consensus and no analyst said it.

What happenedObserved

By 2025 the index reached 5145 — 1255% ahead of what the record at the time implied. This card is the only one on this page built from material filed after January 2010.

Better alternativeCounterfactual

Redeploy the margin now being earned into the next growth vector, before the current one finishes decaying. A lower margin earned more times a year, on the same invested capital.

Why it might have workedCounterfactual

Return on capital in retail is margin times turns, so passing a buying advantage through as price buys traffic, and traffic raises turns on the same fixed store and logistics base.

What could have invalidated itCounterfactual

A business that has just learned to earn a margin is being asked to spend it again, and the market rewarded the margin. The assumption it rests on is that there is a next vector to fund, which the company's own investment and product disclosure at the time either shows or does not — and that was checkable at the time, which is what makes this an argument rather than a preference.

Better alternativeCounterfactual

Press the advantage: fund the next expansion while the cost base is still absorbing the current one. A lower margin earned more times a year, on the same invested capital.
